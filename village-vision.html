Future vision · not yet a physical village

Affordable regenerative village

Belonging should not be a luxury.

We are researching a village model where restored land, simple beautiful homes, shared resources, meaningful work and human care make regenerative living more accessible—not an enclave only for the wealthy.

Where this stands today

The seed is real. The village is future work.

The Living Library, public guides, community channels and Benjy’s land work exist now. No final village site, legal structure, planning permission, capital plan or resident offer has been announced. Those foundations must be researched transparently before invitations or financial commitments are made.

A living lineage of learning

Ancient wisdom. Living practice. Emerging science.

Awakening Eden is not the origin of these ideas. We are part of a much older and wider conversation. We want to learn from living cultures, land-based traditions, regenerative practitioners, ecovillages, farmers, scientists, builders and communities; credit the roots of what we learn; test it humbly in place; and share useful learning back.

Living lineages

Indigenous + ancestral wisdom

Indigenous peoples and living knowledge traditions are cultures and relationships, not content to extract. Where knowledge is shared, we seek consent, attribution, reciprocity, fair exchange and right representation, while keeping each lineage in its own context. We also value local Portuguese, Mediterranean, Celtic and European land traditions carried by living people.

Regenerative practice

Learn from what has already been tried

Permaculture, natural farming, syntropic agroforestry, regenerative agriculture, ecological restoration, intentional communities and long-lived ecovillages offer decades of experiments. We want to weave what works, learn honestly from what failed, credit the projects and teachers, and keep evolving together rather than pretending we invented the pattern.

Science + open inquiry

Curious at the frontier, grounded in evidence

We follow ecology, microbiome and systems biology, soil and water science, and emerging quantum biology. Quantum effects are studied in photosynthetic energy transfer, radical-pair chemistry and molecular tunnelling. We stay curious without using “quantum” as decoration or as scientific proof for spiritual claims the evidence does not establish.

We learn with, not take from.

Co-creation is part of the method.

We do not claim this wisdom as ours. We want to name and link teachers and projects where possible, ask permission when living cultural knowledge is shared, compensate and reciprocate where appropriate, stay open to correction, and contribute our own learning back into the commons.

Zach Bush’s Biological Renaissance, Roots Rural and ORIGIN / ourorigin.earth are contemporary inspirations in this wider field, especially their emphasis on relationship, regenerative action and ancestral wisdom meeting modern science. They are inspirations, not claimed affiliations unless a real partnership is created.

For us, syntropy is also a social and economic question: can this relationship increase the capacity of the whole? We do not want fear, scarcity or zero-sum rivalry to organise the culture. Healthy boundaries and real constraints still matter, but our direction is symbiotic thriving: collaboration, fair exchange, shared learning and enterprises that strengthen one another.

The design brief

Eight relationships to design together.

  • Land as a living trustStewardship structures that protect soil, water, biodiversity and future access beyond one owner’s lifetime.
  • Affordability by designSmaller footprints, shared assets, mixed tenures and transparent costs—not hidden subsidy or unpaid labour.
  • Water, fire and ecology firstRead catchments, geology, access, drought and wildfire before placing buildings or promising productivity.
  • Homes that belong to placeClimate-wise, repairable, healthy materials and modest energy needs, shaped by Portuguese law and local craft.
  • Care across generationsChildren, elders, disability, privacy, conflict support and rest treated as infrastructure, not afterthoughts.
  • Meaningful local livelihoodsFood, education, land care, repair, craft, wellness and digital work held within ecological limits.
  • Capital in service to lifeExplore transparent partnerships with aligned investors, philanthropists and regenerative enterprises so money becomes a tool for restoring water, habitat, biodiversity, community capacity and long-term stewardship—with clear governance before any financial invitation.
  • Commons with clear boundariesShared spaces and resources paired with explicit rights, responsibilities and accountable governance.

Landscape

More life each year

Increasing living cover, infiltration, biomass, soil function, shade, habitat and food resilience while respecting water limits and succession.

Community

Freedom and interdependence

Enough privacy for people to breathe; enough shared life for care, learning, meals, tools and celebration to become easier.

Economy

Access before prestige

Test cooperative, commons, land-trust and hybrid models against real Portuguese law, costs, risks and inclusion outcomes.

A village is a web of relationships.

Research first. Invite carefully. Build in phases.

We will keep evidence, aspirations, decisions and unresolved questions visible as this path develops.
