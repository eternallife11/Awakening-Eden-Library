The principles behind the practice

Work with life. Help the whole place thrive.

There is wonder in watching a landscape come alive. Our part is practical: observe carefully, make thoughtful choices and keep learning from what happens.

Regeneration

Ask what would leave this place healthier: protected soil, more living roots, diverse habitat and a workable rhythm of care. Follow changes in cover, infiltration, plant survival and food production over time.

Permaculture

Design relationships between water, access, gardens, homes and people. Observe before acting; connect useful functions; choose steps that fit the place and the people caring for it.

Explore the design principles →

Syntropic agroforestry

Work with plant communities through space and time: different heights, life cycles and roles, with management that follows succession. Food crops and support plants are planned together, with pruning and biomass care as ongoing work.

Learn from Ernst Götsch’s work →

In Mediterranean landscapes, water availability, summer heat, frost, fire exposure, soil and your capacity to maintain the system guide the design. A planting combination that thrives in tropical Cairns is not automatically suitable here.

The wider regenerative mission

Regenerate the land. Strengthen the people. Protect the living system.

The land work is practical, but it belongs to a bigger commitment: protect water, rebuild living soil, grow food and habitat, restore tired landscapes and help people become confident stewards of the places they care for.

I work with syntropy as a useful design lens: increase life, diversity, cycling, living roots and mutually supportive relationships while reducing waste, bare ground and unnecessary dependence. The goal is not to impose a fashionable template. It is to help the whole property become more resilient, productive and alive over time.

With Sofia, collaborators and the wider Awakening Eden community, I also want to co-create field days, workshops, talks, circles and demonstration projects where people can learn on real land. For us, interbeing is a guiding worldview: inner regeneration, ecological regeneration and healthy community belong in the same conversation, while each practical claim still needs evidence, observation and honest limits.

We want to share solutions and walk our talk. That means doing useful work now, documenting what we learn, connecting good people and helping regenerative projects move from beautiful ideas into living systems.

Central Portugal · concept exploration

Contour Food Forest Ideas

A sloping orchard site explored through two realistic pathways that work with the existing trees rather than erasing what is already there.

  • Option 1: connect existing trees with carefully placed contour planting and infiltration lines.
  • Option 2: create abundance islands around trees with half-moon infiltration features and support species.
  • Shared aim: slow, spread and sink winter rain, build fertility, strengthen tree systems and create phased food-forest abundance.

Concept design only. Final earthworks depend on field-verified contour, slope, soil, rainfall, access and a safe overflow pathway.

Productive ecological restoration

Regeneration can be productive.

Restoration does not have to mean removing people from the landscape.

Beautiful systems can grow food, fertility, habitat, shade, livelihoods and ecological resilience together through agroforestry, perennial crops, orchard systems, mixed production, biomass, living soil, water cycling, succession and diversity.

No universal yield promise. The work begins with context, observation and what the land can realistically support.

The human heart of the work

Why I do this

I love regeneration because it changes the question.

Instead of asking only, “How do we maintain this?” or “How much can we take from it?”, we can ask: How can this place become healthier, more abundant and more alive because we are here?

I love looking at a piece of land and asking: what could this become? Where is the water moving? What is the soil asking for? What trees are already trying to thrive? Where could we grow food, shade, biomass and habitat together?

I love helping someone look at an overwhelming piece of land and suddenly see a pathway. Not a perfect masterplan carved in stone. A living direction. A first step. A system we can keep observing, learning from and improving together.

Ten years of travelling and learning have left me with a deep trust in the goodness of people. Many of us are tired, stretched or living in survival mode. I want this work to offer a little breathing space, useful direction and the feeling that we can make something beautiful together.

That is also why Sofia and I share the Living Library: films, books and real projects that helped us find hope, open our hearts and put our hands to work. There is so much worth caring for. So much we can still create.

My aim is simple: serve people and planet by leaving the land, the people and the project more capable and alive than when we began. We work with syntropy, reciprocity and the understanding that every good intervention should strengthen the wider living system.
