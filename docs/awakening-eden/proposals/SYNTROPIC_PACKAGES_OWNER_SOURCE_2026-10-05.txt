# SYN­TROPIC FOOD FORESTS & ABUNDANCE GARDENS

## From a few young trees to a living ecosystem

Every food forest is different.

Some people want mature fruit trees and an almost-instant garden.

Others want to begin intelligently with earthworks, water, biomass species, pioneer plants, groundcovers and a few carefully chosen productive trees, then allow the ecosystem to grow into abundance over time.

That is why every Awakening Eden system is designed around the land, the budget and the long-term vision.

The biggest variables are:

- size of the system
- mature trees versus young plants
- fruit and specialist species selected
- planting density
- biomass and support species
- irrigation
- soil preparation
- mulch and compost
- earthworks
- contouring and water retention
- fencing and access
- machinery requirements
- whether you want DIY guidance, co-creation or complete installation

## Typical Investment

As a simple guide:

### Small Syntropic Systems
Backyards, small homesteads, demonstration gardens and compact food forests.

**Typical complete investment: €3,500–€6,000**

Often approximately **100–300 m²**.

### Medium Regenerative Gardens
Larger food forests, mixed perennial gardens, raised beds, productive orchards and syntropic rows.

**Typical complete investment: €5,000–€15,000**

Often approximately **300–2,000 m²**.

### Larger Agroforestry Systems
Homesteads, farms, retreat centres, community spaces and regenerative enterprises.

**From €12,000–€15,000+**

Larger projects are individually designed and quoted.

Major infrastructure, large-scale earthworks, tanks, boreholes, extensive fencing, buildings and specialist machinery can be priced separately.

# AN IMPORTANT NOTE ABOUT TIME

A syntropic food forest is not a finished object.

It is a living succession.

## Installation
Most smaller systems can be established in approximately:

**3 days to 3 weeks**

depending on scale and groundwork.

## Year 1
Fast-growing support species begin protecting soil and producing biomass.

Annual vegetables, herbs and pioneer crops can already produce food.

## Years 2–3
Fruit trees establish, soil improves, canopy layers begin forming and biomass cycles accelerate.

## Years 3–5+
The garden increasingly behaves like an ecosystem rather than a planted landscape.

More shade.

More organic matter.

More habitat.

More food.

Less exposed soil.

And increasingly, the possibility of producing fertility from within the system itself.
