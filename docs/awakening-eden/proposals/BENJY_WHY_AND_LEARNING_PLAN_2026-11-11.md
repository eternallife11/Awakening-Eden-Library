## Personal welcome draft — Why I’m creating Awakening Eden

Growing up in Germany, I watched my dad become ill and saw illness touching so many families around us. I kept wondering: *Why? What helps people stay well? What kind of world are we creating—and what could we do differently?*

Those questions eventually led me beyond the supermarket shelves and into gardens, farms, forests and communities.

I began learning about depleted soils, food production, pesticides—including glyphosate-based products such as Roundup—and the relationships between our surroundings and our wellbeing. I also learned that these relationships are complex. No single explanation accounts for every illness. But those questions changed the direction of my life.

Over more than a decade of travelling, volunteering and learning, I discovered people working with life in ways that filled me with hope: protecting soil, growing diversity, returning biomass, caring for water and creating productive landscapes full of relationships.

Imagine children picking fresh leaves from a garden they helped tend. Calendulas flowering beside vegetables. Trees offering shade and habitat. Roots, fungi and countless soil organisms forming relationships beneath our feet.

Not every plant helps every neighbour, and no method works everywhere. That is part of the wonder: we get to observe, learn and become better stewards of the places we belong to.

For me, regeneration is practical—and deeply meaningful. It is compost and curiosity. Thoughtful design and muddy boots. Food, laughter, responsibility and the joy of seeing life return.

**My mission is to inspire, empower and educate—to make these possibilities easier to understand and begin.**

Eden Designer is one contribution to that mission. You don’t need to know everything before starting. Bring your questions, your imagination and your willingness to learn.

Explore our [Living Library](https://awakeningeden.org/living-library). Share your honest experience of the designer. We welcome thoughtful criticism, professional review and collaborators who want to help this work grow.

We’re also nurturing a vision for a regenerative village: a place to live, learn, restore land and explore what a caring community can become.

We are co-creators of the world taking shape through our everyday choices.

**Big love. Enjoy the journey—and let’s grow something life-giving together.**

— Benjy 🌱

## Learning architecture

Keep three frameworks distinct:
1. Permaculture ethics and attributed design principles.
2. Seven proposed Eden foundations (an editorial teaching structure, not a universal scientific standard): observe place; cover soil; maintain living roots; build functional diversity; minimize unnecessary disturbance; cycle biomass and nutrients; care for water and adapt.
3. Syntropic learning: succession, stratification, photosynthesis, biomass management and relationships, adapted to local climate and management.

Use short lessons: situation → why it matters → what to observe → options → one practical action → when to seek help → sources → learn more.
Teach Mediterranean constraints, including summer drought, winter rainfall, fire, access and maintenance. Inspiration remains distinct from measured design. Proposed/existing remains separate from measured/estimated/unknown.
