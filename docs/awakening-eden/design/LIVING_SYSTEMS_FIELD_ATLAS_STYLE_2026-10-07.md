# Awakening Eden — Living Systems Field Atlas

**Educational and concept illustration style, 7 October 2026**  
For the website, Living Library, guides, client concept visuals, workshops and social teaching. This extends the [3 October visual lock](VISUAL_LOCK_2026-10-03.md); it does not replace the approved homepage or Library artwork. The original painted [Library header](references/2026-10-03/living-library-header-original.png), [Library cards](references/2026-10-03/six-library-cards-original.png) and real project photographs are the visual anchors.

## The feeling

**A hand-painted field guide to a more living world.** Warm as an illustrated storybook; accurate enough to teach from; hopeful enough that someone wants to begin. Picture a permaculture designer opening a beloved field notebook beside a garden: ink contours, water routes, roots, leaves, small human gestures and luminous but restrained colour. The drawing reveals *relationships*, not just beautiful objects. It should let a newcomer say, “I understand what changes, why it matters, and what I could do next.”

The reference blend Sofia named is the Earthy Mama warmth of Awakening Eden’s own painted artwork, the legibility of Andrew Millison’s permaculture teaching, and the memorable visual storytelling of After Skool. Borrow those **qualities**, not anyone’s exact composition, characters, marks, lettering or diagrams. Our signature is Mediterranean living-systems knowledge, real human projects, botanical tenderness and design-grade precision. [Andrew Millison](https://www.andrewmillison.com/home) and [After Skool](https://www.afterskool.net/) are inspiration references, not asset sources or implied collaborators.

## The visual recipe

| Layer | Treatment | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Living canvas | Light parchment `#f6f0e2`, warm linen `#e8dec8`, subtle paper grain, generous clear space | Welcome and readable contrast |
| Field drawing | Variable-width umber and deep-forest ink, imperfect but controlled botanical linework | Make landforms, plants, roots and human actions identifiable |
| Watercolour | Transparent olive `#697052`, sage `#94a083`, forest `#344734`, water teal, terracotta `#a7654f`, honey `#b8944b` | Explain function and bring warmth without visual noise |
| Teaching overlay | Clean vector arrows, contours, numbered steps, legends and scale markers | Make the drawing usable as a lesson or design document |
| Spark of wonder | A little honey light, flower, bird, spiral of growth or hand-tended detail where meaningful | Keep the sense of living possibility |

The inks and paint are slightly organic; the *information* is exact. No glossy fantasy landscape, neon “eco” green, heavy fake aging, random sacred geometry or dense decorative border around every diagram. Place ornament at the threshold and corners; give the teaching area breathing room. Keep the existing Cormorant Garamond display, Source Sans 3 body/labels and occasional Caveat field note. Typeset teaching text separately as editable HTML or SVG rather than asking an image model to paint words.

## Three illustration modes

### 1. The teaching plate — one relationship made visible

Use for Library guides and workshops: water moving across a slope, soil cover, a food forest’s layers, a pruning/biomass cycle, or succession through time. One central drawing, one takeaway, ideally three to five labels and one small action. Show above and below ground when roots, infiltration or decomposition matter. Use a restrained arrow language: **teal solid** for water, **ochre curved** for biomass/nutrients, **terracotta numbered** for human actions, **dotted olive** for a longer-term ecological relationship. Include a small legend when two or more flows appear. The arrows must follow plausible physical processes.

Example: a Mediterranean orchard cross-section shows an existing olive retained, living groundcover, mulch around but not against the trunk, flowering shrubs, accessible path, and roots at different depths. A teal rainfall path shows runoff slowed and an overflow route; no promise that one earthwork always works.

### 2. The place vision — a believable future, clearly labelled

Use for Work With Benjy, Work With Us and concept galleries. Begin with a real site photograph, sketch or survey *where available*. The concept drawing shows the same landform, access, existing trees and buildings so visitors recognise the place. Add paths, productive zones, water routes, terraces or beds only when supported by site information. Draw food-forest strata with room for mature crowns, access and maintenance. Show people at human scale using the space: planting, harvesting, resting, learning.

Every concept receives a visible status label: **Existing site**, **Illustrative design concept**, **Proposed phase**, or **Completed project**. A concept beside a real photo is never called a photographic before-and-after. For a proposal, show what may happen in phases rather than pretending instant mature abundance. Use the Luisa and Sim levada-edge rows as a *concept*, Melides and Moinhos as *real work*, and Cairns as a *collaborative tropical research example* with its actual context.

### 3. The living doodle — an idea that moves

Use for short animations, reels and social carousels. A simple hand-drawn story unfolds in three to five frames: **problem seen → living process → possible intervention → care over time → hopeful outcome**. A watering can, worm, seed, person or drop of rain may guide the eye, but avoid a copied whiteboard character style. Draw action with a few deliberate strokes, not dozens of competing icons. End with one invitation: “Watch the rain,” “Cover one patch of soil,” or “Map your trees before planting.”

## What makes it a true permaculture and syntropic design drawing

Start with **climate → landscape → water → access → soil → vegetation → infrastructure → production**. Show contours, slope direction, aspect, existing vegetation and observed drainage when relevant. Mark unknowns instead of inventing them. In Central Portugal and the Mediterranean, consider summer drought, concentrated winter rain, fire, frost pockets, soil depth and long-term irrigation needs. A water-retention idea needs a safe overflow and site assessment; a pond, swale or bio-pool is never a generic decoration.

For a food forest, draw functions as well as species: canopy and sub-canopy, shrubs, herbs, groundcover, climbers, roots, pollinator habitat and biomass/support plants. Show how the system changes through time. A syntropic sequence should distinguish establishment crops and support species from later productive layers, and show pruning/chop-and-drop as managed work. Do not transplant a tropical Cairns pattern directly to a dry Portuguese hillside. Species names and planting combinations require soil, frost, water, spacing and maintenance review before they become client advice.

The ecological story is: **protect the soil → capture more living energy → cycle biomass → build diverse relationships → steward the next stage**. That is a design intention, not a guarantee of yield, groundwater recharge or soil-carbon gain. Where the evidence is observational or a practitioner insight, label it accordingly.

## Composition and reading on a phone

- **Hero/vision image:** one strong scene; leave 25–35% calm area for separately typeset copy. Use a photo when demonstrating completed work, an illustration when opening imagination.
- **Guide diagram:** one question per graphic. Put the action/title outside the image, then one central visual and a short legend beneath. At 390 px, labels should remain comfortably readable; split a dense wide drawing into swipeable stages or vertical panels. Offer tap-to-enlarge and a text explanation.
- **Gallery:** real project photography first, concept visions second, educational drawings third. Captions may be minimal but the status of concepts and examples must remain clear. Avoid presenting a stock or generated scene as Benjy’s completed project.
- **Social carousel:** 4:5 master, five cards at most for one idea. First card poses a felt question; last card offers a feasible action and Library link.
- **Workshop/print:** 16:9 teaching master plus A4/A3 legible export. Keep arrows and text vector-based. Include title, context/location, date, scale or “not to scale,” and source notes where relevant.

Prefer real, readable type to decorative lettering. The painted Library cards are a mood reference, not a template for embedding tiny text in a bitmap. Check colour contrast, keyboard-openable enlargement, alt text, and that a diagram still works if someone cannot distinguish teal from olive.

## Symbols and provenance

The Tree + Heart can appear as a small identity mark. The Lotus of Life must be the **exact 12-fold SVG** at `docs/awakening-eden/design/lotus-of-life-12-exact.svg`, layered separately only when the composition genuinely calls for it. Never request an AI-rendered Lotus, Seed of Life or Flower of Life in a prompt. Educational diagrams usually need no sacred geometry.

For each visual, keep a source note: subject and location; real photograph vs concept vs general example; photographer/creator and permission; collaborators; date/stage; any scientific or practitioner source behind a claim. Do not copy a teacher’s diagram without permission. If using client land, confirm the use of property images and anonymise sensitive details where needed.

## Ready-to-use art directions

**Teaching plate prompt**  
“Create an original Awakening Eden educational illustration: a hand-painted Mediterranean field atlas on light warm parchment, controlled sepia and deep-forest botanical ink, transparent olive/sage and water-teal washes, a little terracotta and honey light. Show **[one specific, reviewed ecological relationship]** in a clear cross-section or plan view, with believable scale, species forms, soil, roots and water behaviour. The image must leave uncluttered spaces for later HTML/SVG labels and arrows. Welcoming, calm, hopeful and practical. No embedded text, no logos, no invented sacred geometry, no photorealistic fake project, no glossy fantasy, no generic tropical vegetation. Use no identifiable composition or character from another artist.”

**Place concept prompt**  
“Create an original Awakening Eden *illustrative design concept* for **[named site and known conditions]**. Use the supplied real site photo/map only as geometry and landform reference. Preserve existing access, buildings, slopes and retained trees. Show a plausible phased regenerative vision with **[approved water, planting and access elements]**, human scale and generous breathing room. Hand-painted field-guide rendering: parchment, fine earthy ink, transparent forest/olive/teal washes, restrained flowering colour. Make the design inviting and professionally legible. Leave a clear area for separate ‘Concept — proposed’ label and annotations. Do not imply the proposal has been built; do not invent site facts or client outcomes. No embedded words or sacred geometry.”

**Five-frame doodle brief**  
“Storyboard one original hand-drawn Awakening Eden explanation of **[process]** in five simple frames: what we observe, why it happens, one appropriate intervention, care over time, and a realistic hopeful possibility. Use living botanical shapes, a human hand where useful, spare earthy ink, sage/teal/terracotta cues and plenty of blank parchment. Each frame teaches one thing. Add the final words as editable type later.”

## First four pieces to make with this system

1. **An orchard can become more than a row of trees:** existing olive/orchard photo, labelled concept layer of soil cover and supportive understory, and a distinct later illustrative vision. No false paired before/after.
2. **How a Mediterranean food forest works:** an improved layered cross-section with plant *roles*, access and root zone, plus a phone-friendly vertical version of the existing board.
3. **A syntropic system through time:** three stages of support planting, biomass management and developing productive strata; distinguish Cairns research from Portuguese adaptations.
4. **What a client actually receives:** a fictional sample set of site-reading map, whole-property concept, planting plan and phased action plan, each clearly marked “sample,” shown next to real Benjy project photos.

## Review before release

Ask five questions: **Is the land and biology plausible? Can someone grasp one idea in ten seconds? Is real work distinguishable from a future concept? Can it be read on a phone? Does it feel like Awakening Eden?** Review planting and water claims with the relevant practitioner or specialist before treating a concept as technical advice. This style is a reusable production brief; no new graphic is approved or published merely because it appears here.
