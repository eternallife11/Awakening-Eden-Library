# Awakening Eden | Regenerative Consulting Design Studio
## Master style guide, AI workflow & release standards

**Version 1.0 | 16 November 2026 | Owner: Benjy / Awakening Eden**  
**Working identity:** A living field journal from a regenerative future.  
**Studio promise:** *See the land clearly. Explain it simply. Restore it responsibly.*

> **The non-negotiable principle**  
> The prettier a map looks, the more carefully we must check that it is truthful. No artificial intelligence may turn a field hypothesis into a surveyed alignment, or a preliminary pond idea into a buildable design.

## 1 | What this studio produces

This system creates authentic, clear, premium regenerative consulting materials for Central Portugal and comparable Mediterranean climates. It serves clients first and sales second. Our reports help people understand **what exists**, **what the land is doing**, **what they hope for**, **what Benjy proposes**, **what remains uncertain**, and **the next responsible action**.

**Deliverable families:**

| Service | Purpose | Appropriate outputs | Not silently included |
|---|---|---|---|
| €111 remote clarity | Remote guidance and next actions | Concise priorities and follow-up notes | Surveyed site map, detailed earthworks |
| €150 initial water/land assessment | Truthful first reading | Field summary; observations map; preliminary concept-and-vision map; 3–5 priorities; follow-up conversation, where agreed | Final contours, construction drawings, engineered road crossings |
| Proposed €350 focused action plan | Return visit, agree and mark 3–5 priorities | Basic on-site levels where feasible, indicative stakes, priority map and phased worksheet | Formal topo/hydraulic survey, excavation, permits, machinery and full planting plan |
| Focused roadmap, generally from €450 online/€650 on-site | More developed chosen area | Refined concept, phasing, researched planting options, budget assumptions | Full engineering or whole-estate implementation |
| Whole-property design, generally from €1,500 | Integrated strategic land planning | Deeper property-wide system and development phases | Automatic permission, engineering sign-off, installation |

These are current working service boundaries, not fixed legal definitions. Contract wording and client-specific agreements take priority. €350 is an **optional proposal**, never a charge automatically added to the €150 visit.

### Source hierarchy / when instructions conflict

1. Benjy and Sofia's most recent explicit decisions for the particular job.
2. Actual client field photos, original survey/base maps, verified measurements and original marked sketches; never overwrite originals.
3. Awakening Eden Project Instructions v3, Canonical Knowledge Map, Superbrain and Regenerative Source of Truth, as applicable.
4. Current Design Bible and Illustration Bible; Content & Image Studio. These are identity references, not licenses to fabricate field data.
5. Independently checked practitioner guidance and current official technical/legal requirements.
6. AI suggestions, stylistic embellishments and marketing convention, always last.

**Evidence protocol:** distinguish `observed`, `documented on supplied plan`, `client wishes`, `Benjy interpretation`, `Benjy proposal`, `illustrative example`, `unverified`, and `specialist review needed`. If the source is missing, say **unknown**.

## 2 | The design identity

### Direction: premium, hand-touched, quiet and usable

Think *a working ecologist's beautiful field journal*, not glossy property advertising and not a mystical infographic. Real landscape photographs are the proof. Precise pencil-like linework helps people see relationships. Painted botanical accents help people feel connected. Page hierarchy makes action obvious.

- **Atmosphere:** warm cream/parchment, forest and olive, moss, soft water teal, honey sunlight, terracotta for bare soil/erosion, a whisper of lavender. Use pigments with organic variation, never neon or slick gradients.
- **Composition:** one strong statement per page, large photographs/maps, generous breathing room, clear captions. Text is left-aligned in an orderly grid. Photos and maps should remain legible on a phone as well as A4 paper.
- **Type:** Cormorant Garamond (short headings), Source Sans 3 (body, captions, maps); Caveat only for one small human note, if useful. If licensed fonts are absent, substitute a high-quality readable serif/sans pairing. Never package or redistribute font files.
- **Photography:** show actual slope, rills, puddles, roots, roads and vegetation. Light natural color balancing and cropping permitted. Do not alter geology, roads, canopy, landform, soil damage, dates or people. Keep original image and an unaltered copy.
- **Illustration:** translucent watercolor washes, restrained dry-brush edges and organic ink. Never use decorative hand-painting to imply a measured flow line. Educational drawings must be spatially plausible and clearly tagged as schematic.
- **Symbols:** no sacred geometry, logo trees, pseudo-topographic flourishes, fake cadastral stamps or decorative scale bars in land-concept pages. If brand geometry is ever truly needed elsewhere, the exact approved 12-fold Lotus of Life is the only source, not a generated approximation.
- **Planting visuals:** draw Mediterranean-appropriate olive, cork oak, fig, almond, citrus, rosemary and lavender as appropriate. Distinguish existing species from proposed species. Do not imply a planting palette is site-approved without soil, exposure, water, fire and availability checks.

### Design as a three-layer object

1. **Truth layer:** locked original photo or georeferenced base, unchanged.
2. **Interpretation layer:** editable SVG/vector overlay with categories and certainty. It should be easy to hide to see the original.
3. **Communication layer:** labels, photos, numbered callouts, legend, narrative and restrained painted texture, never baked into the map source.

**Resolution / file rule:** original maps/photos preserved; layered SVG/HTML/CSS/PPTX/Figma source as appropriate; vector labels; PNG previews; client PDF A4 portrait; overview maps also A3 landscape if useful. Text remains live when possible. Render at print size and phone width. Retain source aspect ratio; never warp a survey.

## 3 | Exactly what each map means

| Map | Contains | Excludes |
|---|---|---|
| **Map A · Existing conditions and observations** | Survey base, visible roads, current vegetation, confirmed site observations, photo points, survey features with status labels, uncertain flow as uncertain | Unapproved pond/swale construction alignments; client dreams posed as built |
| **Map B · Client wishes** | Client's original numbered zones and exact intended uses, spatial context, client-origin status | Hydrological guesses or additions by AI |
| **Map C · Preliminary concept** | Benjy-approved potential swales, planted bands, road protection opportunities, possible basins/pond locations with verification flags and a clear proposed legend | Detailed excavation dimensions, fictitious grades, guaranteed road-to-lake connections |

For a modest €150 report, Map B and Map C may be combined **only** if the legend and visual styles unmistakably separate the client vision from Benjy's preliminary ideas. Never flatten into one uneditable bitmap until final export.

### Map element status and appearance

**Status** controls whether an object is shown; **color** tells what kind of object it is:

- Existing / observed water: solid muted teal with evidence reference.
- Proposed / possible water route: dashed teal with short 'Possible / to verify' label.
- Confirmed erosion: terracotta hatch or compact hazard outline at the observed location.
- Existing road: neutral dark/warm-grey base; proposed side drain: thin grey dashed line, attached to a specific reach and **outlet still to verify**.
- Contour planting: soft olive/moss band, with own evidence/reference status.
- Client numbered vision: lavender markers numbered 1–8, stored independently.
- Pond possibility: translucent organic blue shape only where owner or Benjy explicitly indicated an option, labelled *possible*. Never auto-fill valleys with pretty oval ponds.
- No known safe outlet: **stop symbol / 'outlet to verify'**. Do not draw a blue line continuing to a lake merely to complete the picture.

**Line semantics:** flow-direction arrows and swales are different objects. A swale lies on verified contour and holds/infiltrates water; a graded channel conveys water downhill to a tested outlet. 'Zig-zag' in a sketch communicates the intention of distributed slowing, **not** measured geometry.

### Spatial accuracy: image registration checklist

- Use the high-quality original base map, never a compressed WhatsApp preview if the original is available.
- Read north, parcel, contour legends, coordinate labels and the difference between proposed/existing features from the source plan.
- Fix the same crop/orientation/aspect for every map layer. If sketches were made on a screenshot, align using visible control points before transferring them; document any mismatch.
- Maintain a source manifest: `object_id`, `type`, `status`, `source_file`, `source_page_or_photo`, `geometry_basis`, `approved_by`, `last_changed`, `uncertainties`.
- Do not trace from perspective field photos as if they were top-down contour survey. Put photo concepts on the photo and true plan alignments on the map only when anchored.
- Show a real north arrow or scale only if valid for that exact base and crop; otherwise omit rather than invent.
- Do not move the printed reservoir, client numbers, roads, boundaries, building footprints or photo points just to improve composition.

## 4 | Ecological reasoning and hydrological safety

### What Benjy's concept is trying to achieve

**Begin upslope, protect living soil, prevent prolonged road flow, distribute appropriate water into suitable ground, and confirm safe overflow before any digging.** This is a *strategy*, not a promise that every swale, pond or trench will be safe.

**Do not confuse these tools:**

| Tool | Function | First question |
|---|---|---|
| Planted contour strip / brush line | Reduce sheet erosion, reinforce groundcover, build biomass | Will it intercept or concentrate water; is the slope stable? |
| Level swale | Temporarily collect and infiltrate runoff | Is the slope suitable, soil infiltrative and overflow safely designed? |
| Roadside side drain | Convey road runoff in a protected channel | Where does each short reach discharge, and will the outlet survive? |
| Rolling dip / cross-road canalete / culvert | Remove water from roadway at specific point | Ownership, traffic load, gradients, inlet/outlet protection, sediment and approvals? |
| Sediment cell | Trap transported soil with safe cleanout | Is there access for maintenance and controlled overflow? |
| Infiltration basin | Store a shallow temporary pulse and infiltrate | What about soakage, seasonal wetness, slope stability and overflow? |
| Storage/habitat pond | Retain water over longer periods | Water source, sealing, bank stability, emergency spillway, permits and maintenance? |
| Natural swimming pool | Clean bathing water and ecological recreation | How is potentially contaminated road runoff kept completely separate? |

**Absolute hold points:** no under-road excavation, load-bearing road works, impoundment, dam/bund across drainage, steep-slope water-retention excavation, watercourse intervention, new overflow onto neighbors or water transfer to unstable land until measured, designed and authorized as required. Do not imply the €350 basic level/staking visit is an engineering survey.

**Prefer life-serving first actions:** observe during rainfall; keep existing safe drains working; protect exposed bare soil; use small removable surface brush / mulch / fiber controls *only where they cannot dam a flow channel or create a road hazard*; preserve roots; reduce sediment movement; plan native/adapted planting with fire-wise placement. Evaluate soil cover and photosynthesis alongside engineered options. Works may require owner/road authority/Câmara/APA input; verify based on actual location and status.

## 5 | The permanent production workflow (one gate at a time)

**Use this order. Do not combine design, technical analysis and PDF export into one giant prompt.** Save the output and review of each stage before moving on.

| Stage | The decision / product | Approval gate |
|---|---|---|
| **00 Intake** | File list, originals, scope, client identity and privacy audit | Complete assets and permissions? |
| **01 Evidence** | Claims/evidence register; confirmed/unknown and photo inventory | Benjy confirms what he actually observed |
| **02 Site reading** | Draft hydrology, risk questions, natural assets, no proposed edits | No invented flow or levels |
| **03 Vision** | Client wishes as supplied, numbers and descriptions unchanged | Names, zones and needs correct |
| **04 Concept brief** | Candidate interventions with rationale, dependencies and safe outlet unknowns | Benjy approves exact ideas and rough locations |
| **05 Map overlays** | A/B/C editable SVG or equivalent, base unchanged | Geometry traced to approved source |
| **06 Photo annotation** | Original photos with editable pencil overlays | Every arrow/callout matches source |
| **07 Education art** | Standalone swale, road and pond cross-sections, marked 'illustrative' | Logical but non-site-specific drawings |
| **08 Writing & layout** | Short narrative, map-first report, warm note and service scope | Truth, clarity, approved commercial language |
| **09 Render & visual QA** | Full-page renders, phone-width checks, map closeups and fixes | Zero critical failures |
| **10 Human review/release** | Client PDF, editable masters, traceability ledger, approved sign-off | Benjy explicitly approves before sending |

### Good deliverable order for client reading

1. Photograph-led cover with title; **no incorrect feature lines on hero**.
2. Human note from Benjy: gratitude, what actually struck him, what comes next.
3. Site's water story and observed problems, supported by tagged real photos.
4. Map A: current conditions, observation photo references and unresolved points.
5. Map B: the client's goals, retained numbers.
6. Map C: preliminary water-first concept with tentative lines and stop points.
7. Two or three educational illustrations, clearly not to scale.
8. Priorities: 3–5 sequential actions, and what's safe to prepare now versus what requires surveys or approvals.
9. Optional next-phase visit and separate implementation options.
10. Technical uncertainties, maintenance responsibilities and invitation to correct the interpretation.

**Editorial formula:** each section answers one question, e.g. *What did you see? Why does it matter? What are the options? What must we check? What is the next action?*

## 6 | Prompt architecture for ChatGPT and Claude

The best prompting is not 'think harder and make it magical'. Give a **role**, **source list**, **purpose**, **approved constraints**, **one stage**, **deliverables**, **stop conditions**, and **tests**. Use named sections or XML for long prompts. The prompt should stand alone if opened in a fresh session.

**Reusable wrapper:**

```text
<role>Regenerative land design analyst + careful visual production partner.</role>
<project>Awakening Eden / [Client] / €[scope] / Central Portugal.</project>
<source_material>Only the attached and indexed originals and approved decisions.</source_material>
<job_stage>[00..10]. Complete this stage only.</job_stage>
<invariants>
No invented hydrology, site data, structures, pond sites or permissions.
Never alter original field photographs. Keep maps and graphics editable.
Separate existing, observed, client wish, proposed, unknown and illustrative.
Use the current design/illustration bibles; no sacred geometry in consulting maps.
</invariants>
<output>[Named files and expected formats].</output>
<verification>[Checks that must pass; list unknowns].</verification>
<stop_condition>Stop after this gate and provide a concise review pack. No sending, publishing or deployment.</stop_condition>
```

**Why two AI roles?** ChatGPT (or another research-oriented model) can compare interpretation and prepare editorial/creative briefs; Claude Code excels at controlled file operations, SVG/HTML layout, repetitive QA and building the export pipeline. Neither replaces Benjy's direct field judgment or a qualified survey/engineering professional. A model that paints illustrative images must not be delegated map geometry.

**Use reference roles in image prompts:** `Image 1 = original photo (preserve)`; `Image 2 = approved hand sketch (only annotation shape)`; `Image 3 = styling reference (pigment texture only)`; `Image 4 = base map (geometry context)`. Never treat a styled inspiration image as evidence.

### Recommended use of Claude Code itself

- Keep **`CLAUDE.md` short**: project-specific non-negotiables and how to run QA. Don't paste this entire manual there.
- Put this procedure in a **reusable project skill** `.claude/skills/eden-concept-report/SKILL.md`, with step prompts as references; invoke intentionally.
- Work in a *copy or feature branch*, never overwrite the existing client work or source images.
- Ask Claude to **explore first** and produce a short plan and file/change list **before generating layouts**.
- After each stage inspect actual PNG/PDF renders, not just its verbal summary. Compare screenshots against the approved overlay.
- Use a read-only reviewer or separate fresh review session for the evidence and map QA. A second perspective helps, but does not establish truth.
- Use `/design` and `/design-sync` for visual exploration only if supported in the client's Claude Code environment; actual field-map geometry remains in traceable layers even if a design canvas is used.
- Keep no unnecessary MCP connectors active. Don't let the agent send emails, deploy a website, push private photos or post client materials without consent.

## 7 | Twelve copy-ready stage prompts

> The pack also provides each prompt as an individual file in `03_Step_Prompts/`. Paste **one** per stage after checking the previous gate.

### 00 · Inspect and plan

```text
Read the Awakening Eden Master Style Guide, source-of-truth instructions and the existing project. Do not edit anything yet. Inventory all files, note version conflicts, identify the approved source drawings and the current report's errors. Show a numbered plan for stages 00–10, a list of files you will read/write, and any missing source. Stop for approval. Do not infer facts from filenames alone.
```

### 01 · Evidence ledger

```text
Create EVIDENCE_LEDGER.csv and PHOTO_MANIFEST.md. For every site claim, record the exact source file, observation, certainty, client/Benjy origin, rough spatial reference and missing verification. Distinguish a visible rill from your inferred flow direction; distinguish proposed reservoir from built reservoir. If a photo location is not anchored to a map, mark location unknown rather than placing a misleading point. Output only ledger, uncertainties and 5 critical verification questions. No design yet.
```

### 02 · Existing conditions / Map A

```text
From the unaltered base map plus confirmed field evidence, prepare Map A. Copy known boundaries, roads, built structures and marked survey features exactly. Add only verified photo points and visible issues. For uncertain routes use explicit 'reported / inferred; check in rain'. Leave proposed trenches, swales and ponds OFF this map. Deliver editable layered source, exported preview and a per-object source list. Stop for approval.
```

### 03 · Client vision / Map B

```text
Reproduce the owners' numbered vision precisely, without adding new facilities: 1 natural swimming pool; 2–3 orchard (about 30–40 fruit trees, list pending); 4 vegetable garden; 5 picnic under existing trees; 6 cactus garden; 7 animals/sanctuary; 8 food/machinery storage plus some animals/stall. Treat all as intended land uses unless verified as built. Use the supplied hand-marked plan. Keep the client-vision layer lavender and separate. Export a clean map and short legend. Stop.
```

### 04 · Candidate water strategy review

```text
Explain the strategy from upper catchment/upper forest roads toward lower receiving areas. Create a table: candidate, intended benefit, supporting source, physical mechanism, hazard/false assumption, measurements needed, permit/owner consent, safe outlet status, Benjy approval status. Compare (a) living cover/brush lines, (b) site-suitable contour infiltration, (c) short roadside drains and safe cross-road outlets, (d) potential sediment cells/basins/ponds. RED FLAG any channel that stops without a stable outlet, a swale off contour or pond that is only AI invention. No drawing yet.
```

### 05 · Map C / approved ideas only

```text
Use the candidate list Benjy explicitly approved and the source sketches at their exact relative locations. Put preliminary lines on a transparent editable vector overlay aligned to the locked base map. Preserve source sketch endpoints and bends; do not add decorative loops. For every line include ID, origin/source image, type, status, approximate-only note, outlet to verify and review field. Use dashed segments for proposed alignments. If no geometry has enough evidence, add a callout on the side instead of a fabricated line. Export SVG, PNG preview, and a line-by-line audit table. Stop for geometry review.
```

### 06 · Photo-annotation master

```text
Build photo annotations as transparent SVG overlay layers on the untouched JPG/PNG originals. Use the approved field sketches, matching scale/rotation/crop. Preserve landmarks and locations. Use no more than 2–4 callouts per photo. Type labels with live text. Show original and annotated side by side; make sure overlays follow exactly what was drawn. If user-approved sketch is missing, show no new arrows. Output edit-friendly master + preview grid. Stop.
```

### 07 · Educational diagrams / illustrations

```text
Create THREE separate reusable conceptual vector illustrations in the field-journal style: (1) on-contour planted swale with safe overflow, (2) a road cross-drain / rolling dip with protected receiving area, (3) three pond typologies: infiltration basin, sediment cell, storage/habitat pond, and a separate note that natural swimming water must be protected from dirty road runoff. Do not draw site dimensions or claim these are built on this client's property. Use plausible elevations/flow arrows, text on separate editable layer, plants rooted in soil, and gentle watercolor accents beneath accurate vector paths. Export SVG and PNG. Validate diagrams with a technical QA pass.
```

### 08 · Editorial narrative and layout

```text
Use the approved ledger, Map A, B, C, concept review and field photographs to compose a concise A4 portrait report. Make every page answer one client question. Benjy's note should sound warm, grounded and first-person. Real photos as hero evidence, small sketches for explanation, short legends. The €150 package is the current report; a separate €350 focused return/staking visit is optional and not an engineering survey; labour/machine/materials extra. Use approved brand typography/color sources, no false precision. Supply PDF proof and editable source. Do not send or publish.
```

### 09 · QA self-test and independent review

```text
Run the QA checklist provided in 04_QA_and_Forms. Render all PDF pages as images; inspect every page at 100% and at phone width. Compare Map A/B/C and every photographed site issue against originals; enumerate every vector line with its source and outlet status. Check SVG layer editability, missing assets, text clipping, legibility, privacy, quotes and price scope. Then ask a separate read-only reviewer to challenge evidence and hydrology (no editing). Make a PASS/FAIL report and fix failures, rendering again. Never claim completion without actual files and checks.
```

### 10 · Client approval and handoff

```text
Prepare a concise human-review packet: final proof PDF, 3 separate map PNGs, edit-friendly SVG/source, photo evidence index, outstanding site/legal questions, commercial scope, short client message draft and revision log. Clearly mark 'NOT YET APPROVED'. Wait for Benjy to check uncertain lines, safety and wording. Do not send, post, merge or publish. After explicit approval, produce the agreed export name and archive the approved version, never overwriting raw sources.
```

### 11 · Repeatable post-project learning

```text
Create a retrospective with: top three things the client understood; confusion points; map geometry mistakes prevented; missing field photos; practical design decisions validated on site; what needed a specialist; layout improvements; changes to reusable components. Update the STYLE_CHANGELOG for Benjy's review. Never rewrite the canonical Bible based only on one generated example or unreviewed AI preference.
```

## 8 | Art-direction and image prompts that actually help

**Generate illustrative education art, not 'satellite evidence'.** For a future garden/quinta vision, original artwork can show a plausible but explicitly fictional scene. For an actual client site, use photo + vector compositing, not image regeneration.

### Master painted schematic prompt (image model)

```text
Create one educational regenerative land diagram, not a map of any real property. Subject: [one mechanism]. Medium: premium painterly watercolor and soft dry-brush ink on cream paper; warm Central Portugal afternoon, olive, sage, honey and a little lavender; precise section logic with earth, soil roots, planted cover and restrained blue water. Layout: [cross-section or oblique diagram], ample empty areas for editable labels to be added outside the generated art. Respect realistic water direction and gravity. No words, prices, logos, fake topographic data, scale bars, measurements, people, palms or tropical vegetation. No sacred geometry. No implied approved construction. Deliver clean art; labels and final arrows will be added as vector elements after technical review.
```

### Approved sketch to vector overlay prompt (Claude Code, **not** generative image)

```text
Use ORIGINAL_PHOTO as an immutable background; APPROVED_MARKUP as the sole guide to annotation geometry. Digitize the markup as one or more editable SVG paths on a transparent layer, preserving relative endpoints, kinks and bounding region. Do not invent, smooth into a new route, close a pond polygon, add arrows, move objects, recolor soil, or paint the photo. Use STYLE_REFERENCE only for line stroke appearance, not geometry. Provide a 50%-opacity comparison proof, an overlay-only SVG, a source-to-path table and an unaltered original.
```

### Gentle photo-plus-paint header prompt

```text
Design a report cover using the attached genuine site photograph. Keep original landscape features and evidence unaltered. Crop sensitively, correct only basic exposure/white balance and reserve a quiet cream text zone outside important site features. Add at most a small restrained pigment brush accent; DO NOT put water arrows, proposed swales, ponds, drainage lines or markers on the cover. Use live text: project title, 'Initial Water & Land Assessment / Preliminary Concept', Benjy / Awakening Eden. Proof on phone width and A4.
```

### Why clean linework beats 'AI magic'

For maps and client-owned hand sketches, SVG overlays should be authored or traced reproducibly in code. Generative raster tools may distort the route, remove trees or invent ponds. Use generated watercolor only as an *underpainting* or independent explanatory artboard. For technical illustrations, use real/vector geometry first, then gentle painted texture; put arrows and labels after technical approval.

## 9 | Lessons from the Adam & Sandra proof (our regression test)

The current 13-page draft demonstrates both the strengths of the system and what we must prevent:

- **Page 1:** the cover uses a real slope photo with water/swale markings that can imply exact lines. For a first client report, make the cover clean. Move all linework to a titled, traceable concept plate.
- **Pages 3 and 9:** the most sensitive items are positions of A–F, drawn water pathways and possible pond locations. Run a point-by-point source audit against the original plans and Benjy's field sketches. If a geometry source is missing, mark it *unplaced / to confirm* instead of guessing.
- **Pages 5–6:** road protection is a strong educational topic. Distinguish an actual photographed rill from a conceptual canalete or crossing. Any generic spacing table from a road manual is only a reference, **not** a prescription for this Portuguese road.
- **Pages 7–8:** concept swales and three pond types work as educational illustrations if distinctly separated from site proposals. The upper pond near the future store or forest track remains to verify, not a default decorative shape.
- **Page 10:** client vision numbers 1–8 must retain original meaning. Natural pool is a wish, the printed rainwater reservoir may be only a proposal; a swimming pool does not receive contaminated road runoff.
- **Pages 11–13:** keep first actions conditional, distinguish the €150 deliverable from optional €350 visit, and keep permits/engineer responsibilities visible but not alarmist.

**Correction loop:** log the mistake with screenshot, affected file, true source, fix, map-layer ID and reviewer. Render the repaired page; compare side by side. Update this guide only when a repeatable rule is learned and Benjy approves.

## 10 | Quality assurance: hard gates before a client ever sees it

A beautiful PDF can still be wrong. Use the companion `QA_RELEASE_CHECKLIST.md` and submit the completed review record every time.

**Gate 1: source fidelity** — all original photos and maps are unchanged, asset count documented, correct client numbers, exact quotes, no fabricated measurement/feature, known/unknown statuses visible.

**Gate 2: hydrological coherence** — every line has a type, evidence and outlet condition; swales are not sloping drain lines; no proposed retention on unstable/wet ground without verification; no new off-site discharge, no dirty water into swimming, no assumed pond safety; maintenance considered.

**Gate 3: spatial fidelity** — base orientation and aspect preserved; approved geometry transcribed accurately; leader lines correspond to known photo points; no A–F point moved without proof; layer list is complete; all dashed/solid meanings match the legend.

**Gate 4: design and educational clarity** — client understands the water journey in under one minute; biggest map readable without pinching excessively; evidence photographs large; captions answer a question; palette restrained; art labeled illustration; no small/faint type; information dominates decoration.

**Gate 5: delivery and commercial truth** — €150 and optional €350 explicitly separated; work and dates not promised before approval; project scope and permissions stated; final PDF rendered page-by-page, no clipped/overlapping text; private phones and client data redacted where unnecessary; no auto-send.

**Release statuses:** `DRAFT` -> `EVIDENCE REVIEWED` -> `BENJY CONCEPT APPROVED` -> `LAYOUT PROOF` -> `QA PASSED` -> `CLIENT APPROVED FOR SENDING`. No skipping the stages.

**Stop-ship criteria:** invented pond; unknown outlet shown as safe; wrong point/parcel; plan proposal shown as built; unsupported flow direction; incorrectly promised engineering or approvals; severe text clipping; lost original photo; accidental privacy leakage. Any one is a FAIL.
