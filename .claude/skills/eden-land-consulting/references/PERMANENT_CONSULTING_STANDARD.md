# Awakening Eden · Permanent Regenerative Consulting Standard
Version: 2026-11-16; all clients; Central Portugal/Mediterranean by default. This is a professional working method, not a license to perform unqualified engineering.

## 0. Purpose and voice
Regenerative Eden Landscapes / Work with Benjy helps landowners understand a living place and take informed, beautiful, practical action. Make clients more capable: show them where water, soil, sun, wind, access, vegetation, people and ecology meet. Write in Benjy's natural voice: warm, first-hand, clear, direct, occasionally poetic, never inflated, manipulative or overly technical. Do not pretend Benjy personally observed something that came only from a plan or a third party.

## 1. Evidence first: six immutable categories
Record every statement/mark with a source ID, author, type, certainty and timestamp.
- **OBSERVED**: Benjy's field photograph, video, field note, clearly documented condition.
- **SUPPLIED PLAN**: topography, cadastral, architectural or satellite drawing, with source date / legend and reliability. Planned != built.
- **CLIENT VISION**: aspirations, numbered areas, preferred species, budget, animals, phasing. Not existing site features.
- **BENJY PROPOSAL**: suggested priority, conceptual pond / contour / water line. Provisional unless surveyed/approved.
- **ILLUSTRATION / PRECEDENT**: explanation of how a swale or road drain works; not a measured property map or promised outcome.
- **UNKNOWN / TO VERIFY**: flow direction, slope, bearing, outlet, capacity, soil infiltration, permit, buried services, contractor quote.
Never let a line migrate from one category into another without a recorded review/approval. No anonymous map lines.

## 2. Design process (inspired by SADI / OBREDIM / permaculture design cycle)
**Survey/Observe → Analyse → Options/Design → Implement → Monitor/Evaluate → Adapt**. Work backward from the client's aims without ignoring site constraints. Use Yeomans' scale of permanence as a *thinking aid* (climate, landscape/water, access, trees, structures, divisions, soil), not a rigid algorithm. Use zones/sectors and relative location. Return to observation after rainfall and across seasons.

### Survey the people
Ask for their real goals, urgent problems, current land use, desired abundance, wildlife values, budgets/time/skills, access needs, animals, children, neighbours, maintenance willingness, priority season and decision authority. Clarify first-step scope. Never label client ideas as approved construction.

### Survey the place (broad to detailed)
- **Land/legal:** boundaries and access rights, slopes, ridge/valley lines, elevation confidence, geology, cut/fill, retaining structures, past land disturbance, designated protections, utilities, ownership and works permissions.
- **Water:** rain origin/catchment (including above boundary), roof/road runoff, drains/culverts, watercourses, springs, observed flow and pooling, infiltration/soil saturation, rills/gullies, outlet destination, downstream neighbours, flood and seasonal extremes. **Trace every proposed flow from source to safe receiving path and exceedance overflow.** Never infer slopes or capacity from map appearance alone.
- **Soil:** texture/structure, compaction, infiltration observations, organic matter, roots, depth to rock, signs of erosion, contamination risks, tested vs visual observations, soil-cover status, biological activity.
- **Climate/sectors:** seasonal sun/shade/orientation, frost hollows, heat, prevailing winds, rain direction, wildfire exposure/access, summer drought, winter storm response, neighbouring influences and views/noise.
- **Living assets:** existing mature trees, native vegetation, beneficial corridors, habitats, natural regeneration, invasive risks, biomass, woody debris, fire fuel continuity and planted vs unplanted areas.
- **Human infrastructure:** roads/tracks, grading, buildings/roofs, fences, water sources, storage, irrigation, waste, electrical services, machinery access, footpaths and maintenance routes.
- **Use systems:** food growing/orchards, syntropic layers and succession, livestock/animal sanctuary, structures, habitat, recreation, education and neighbours.
Collect north, approximate scale/provenance and photo positions when reliable. Unknown measurements remain **UNKNOWN**; a dimension inferred from pixel width is not valid without calibration.

### Analysis and options
Identify the 3–5 most important current limiting factors and interdependencies. Compare non-earthwork/low-risk options with earthwork options. Understand how water is already managed by living systems. Rank proposals by (a) urgency/risk; (b) upstream leverage; (c) fit with owners' vision; (d) safe outlet; (e) reversibility/cost/labour; (f) seasonal timing; (g) long-term stewardship. Explain trade-offs, not only a favourite solution.

### Water-first design safeguards
- Start analysis at the upstream catchment, but **do not automatically retain water at the absolute highest point**. Assess infiltration, landslide/erosion susceptibility, steepness, existing drainage and downstream consequences.
- Swales are level/contour infiltration features *only on suitable stable ground*; they require surveyed lines, appropriate overflow and side slopes; a downslope diversion is NOT a swale.
- Roads should shed runoff frequently through safely located grade reversals, drains/canaletes, or crossings, with protected receiving areas. A long continuous trench can create a larger concentrated flow, and crossing positions need evaluation.
- Stone/gravel conveyance and planted channels do different jobs. Gravel can clog, need maintenance and does not by itself make a drainage channel safe. Select after runoff load, soil/grade/outlet assessment.
- Infiltration basin, sediment forebay and storage/habitat pond are different; water quality matters. Don't put dirty road runoff directly into swimming-water systems or animal drinking water.
- **No built pond/dam/spillway/road-cut design from photos alone.** Verify elevations, soils, catchment, inflows, spillways/overflow, stability and possible specialist engineering. Check road owner, APA/Câmara and other applicable legal constraints before earthworks.
- Avoid concentrating water beside foundations, unstable road embankments, neighbours or steep slopes; check lower/outlet areas early.
- Keep soil covered: existing vegetation, living roots, biomass, suitable seasonal cover, careful fire-aware design. Don't plant moisture-loving tropical species by default in Mediterranean dry summers.

**Latest Benjy/Sofia client agreements take priority. Confirm specific price/deliverables before publication. These are the recommended working defaults as of 2026-11-16.**

State observations are remote/unverified. No surveyed alignment, construction detail, fixed plant schedule or engineered water system.

### €150 Local Water & Land Assessment (first field reading)
Includes scoped local field observation; a short summary; main issues/opportunities, **priority areas and concise written action plan**, map(s) showing existing observations and preliminary proposals/client wishes. No survey-grade contour lines, equipment specs, permit guarantee, bill of quantities, detailed planting palette or detailed construction roadmap. Does not include formal topo/hydro/geotechnical survey, engineered waterworks, permits, paid labour, digger hire or implementation; define exact call/visit time and travel. ### From €450 Online / from €650 On-Site Focused Regenerative Roadmap

### From €1,500 Whole-Property Regenerative Design
Integrated master concept across water, access, sectors, zones, soil, vegetation, food production, animals, habitat, infrastructure, phases, maintenance and indicative investment strategy. Multiple linked maps and planting/service systems as agreed. An integrated masterplan ≠ engineering drawings or building approvals; define scope/fees before start. Add installation/project management by separate quote.

**Rule:** Never withhold a material danger to sell an upgrade. Progression is increased precision, integration, design effort, quantities, coordination, iteration and support, not secret knowledge.

## 4. Required client deliverables by scope
All tiers: one-paragraph problem/vision summary; source/evidence limitation; clear map legend; 3–5 priorities where appropriate; short written action plan; suggested next step; scope boundary. Use real photos only and cite source when public factual claim matters.
- Entry: readable overview concept, 1–2 maps/layers as agreed, simple why/where/what-now, concise area guidance, no fake exactness.
- Focused: greater detail in ONE area, alternatives, staged planting/earthwork *concept*, follow-up decisions and maintenance.
- Master: integrated landscape relationships and more complete detailed design phases, sourced palette/quantities conditional on measurements.

## 5. Planting and succession layer
Design from functions: hold soil, build biomass, foster infiltration, improve microclimate, support beneficial life, human yield, firewise buffers. Use native and appropriate Mediterranean support plants and proven practitioner selections, verified by elevation, frost, water, nursery availability, invasiveness and legislation. Discuss support species / pioneer biomass, root patterns, succession and strata; plan winter establishment and spring/summer survivability with irrigation strategy. Draw tree positions only when approved and appropriately scaled; otherwise use conceptual planting bands. Orchard and food forest composition belongs to client preferences and deeper design as needed, never invented.

## 6. Map set
- Map A: sourced existing conditions & observations (no proposed lines).
- Map B: owner vision and numbered areas (numbering faithfully preserved).
- Map C: preliminary concept interventions with approval/status layer and priority legend. **For small packages, A+B+C may be combined into TWO simpler readable maps or separate layers**, provided source categories are unmistakable.
- Detailed map/planting plan only in appropriately scoped deeper stages. No arbitrary pond ovals, route arrows, unverified water destinations, compass values or scales.
- Every map line needs feature ID, source file, status, approval flag, purpose, implied direction, proposed destination/outlet and any pending verification. Source line and styled drawn line should be comparable at known landmarks; use editable SVG groups or GIS layers.

## 7. Report editorial order
Natural field-photo cover (no invented hero lines) → short note from Benjy → what land/owners ask for → what was observed → working maps → water/soil/sun/wind logic → proposed choices & tradeoffs → 3–5 priorities & early actions → owners' vision integration → optional deeper scope → what remains unmeasured / approvals. Prefer short generous captions over walls of text. Label all illustrations **not the client's actual land**.

## 8. Required human review checkpoints
1. Inventory and quote/scope accuracy.
2. Evidence register and existing-conditions map.
3. Benjy-approved owner vision / candidate interventions before styling the lines.
4. Map geometry verification and representative photo annotation.
5. Actual PDF preview on phone + print; legal/safety notes.
6. Client-facing release (only after explicit approval).

## 9. Safety and legal research
For Portugal use primary sources for road ownership, land-use restrictions, APA/ARH watercourse works and Câmara Municipal permissions; request qualified civil/geotechnical/hydrology help when relevant. Don't state a specific work is legal or exempt based only on general laws. Example official sources: https://apambiente.pt/agua/licenciamento and https://diariodarepublica.pt/dr/legislacao-consolidada/lei/2005-34506275 . Re-check current status for every actual site.

## 10. Final quality question
Is the report true to the land and its people, internally consistent, practical, life-serving, safe enough to propose, editable and beautiful? Does it leave people more capable rather than falsely certain?
